The two main concepts to be applied here is conservation of energy and conservation of momentum in relativistic contexts.

Beginning with conservation of momentum, we know that in relativistic contexts, $p=\gamma mv^2$, thus we can just equate the momentum of $M_{1}$ to the momentums of $M_{1}$ and $M_{2}$. 
So:
$$
\gamma_{1} M_{1}v_{1}^2=\gamma_{2} M_{2}v_{2}^2+\gamma_{3} M_{3}v_{3}^2
$$
Computing each of the gammas using this formula:
$$
\gamma = \frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1-\frac{v^2}{c^2} }}
$$
$$
\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1-\frac{\left( \frac{5c}{13} \right)^2}{c^2} }}
$$
$$
\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{ \frac{12}{13}} = \frac{13}{12}
$$

$$
\gamma_{2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1-\frac{0^2}{c^2} }}=1
$$

$$
\gamma_{3}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{ 1-\frac{\left( \frac{12c}{13} \right)^2}{c^2} }}=\frac{13}{5}
$$
Inputting alongside the velocities:
$$
\frac{13}{12}M_{1}\left( \frac{5c}{13} \right)^2=0+\frac{13}{5}M_{3}\left( \frac{12c}{13} \right)^2
$$
$$
\frac{1}{12}(1440)\left( \frac{5}{13} \right)=\frac{12}{5}M_{3}\left( \frac{1}{13} \right)
$$
$$
25(120)=\frac{12}{5}M_{3}
$$
$$
M_{3}=250MeV/c^2
$$

Now, computing the energy conservation which uses the formula: $\gamma mc^2$
$$
\gamma_{1}M_{1}c^2=\gamma_{2}M_{2}c^2+\gamma_{3}M_{3}c^2
$$
We have already acquired the $\gamma$ values from above so inputting them:
$$
\frac{13}{12}(1440)c^2=M_{2}c^2+\frac{13}{5}M_{3}c^2
$$

$$
13(120)=M_{2}+\frac{13}{5}M_{3}
$$
Inputting the $M_{3}$ from above:
$$
13(120)=M_{2}+\frac{13}{5}(250)
$$
$$
M_{2}=910MeV/c^2
$$
Thus the solution is:
$$
\boxed{M_{2}=910MeV/c^2, M_{3}=250MeV/c^2}
$$
