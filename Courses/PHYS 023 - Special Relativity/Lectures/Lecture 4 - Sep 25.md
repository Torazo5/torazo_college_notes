something is really odd here cuz mass isn't conserved
mass isn;t conserved in this calculation and we know this is true

more precisely: the **sum of the rest masses** isn't conserved
- before: $M + m = \frac{16}{9}m + \frac{9}{9}m = \frac{25}{9}m$
- after: $M_f = \frac{35}{9}m$
- the extra $\frac{10}{9}m$ is the kinetic energy the two guys brought in, and now it's rest energy ($\Delta M_f c^2 = K_{before}$) stored as internal energy (heat, deformation, etc.) of the stuck-together blob

when does this happen?
- **inelastic collision** (KE not conserved): sum of rest masses changes. KE lost → rest mass goes up (like this one). Rest mass lost → KE goes up (decays, fission, fusion)
- **elastic collision** (KE conserved): sum of rest masses stays the same

what IS always conserved: total energy $E = \sum \gamma m c^2$ and total momentum $\vec p = \sum \gamma m \vec v$
- also the **invariant mass of the whole system**, $M_{sys}c^2 = \sqrt{E_{tot}^2 - (p_{tot}c)^2}$. Here $p_{tot} = 0$, so $M_{sys} = E_{tot}/c^2 = \frac{35}{9}m$ both before AND after. The system's mass is conserved; mass just isn't additive (you can't add up the parts' rest masses to get the whole)
