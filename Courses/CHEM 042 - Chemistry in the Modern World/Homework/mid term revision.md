### Quick review about splitting / the n+1 rule

> [!important]
> $n$ is NOT just the group next door
> $n$ = total count of every proton within $\leq 3$ bonds, added up across all groups

two exclusions:
- protons equivalent to the one im watching dont count (the other H's on my own carbon)
- only valid as one $n+1$ if the J's are similar, otherwise multiply $(n_{1}+1)(n_{2}+1)$

**example** CH3-CH2-CH2Br, looking at the middle CH2
3 bonds to the CH3 (3H) and 3 bonds to the CH2Br (2H)
so $n = 3+2 = 5$ -> sextet
the CH3 and the CH2Br are 4 bonds from each other so they dont split each other, only the middle one sees both

> [!warning]
> also remember n+1 gives lines INSIDE one signal, not n+1 signals

## Summary

Midterm revision notes, in two halves. The first half is the things to remember: same-element bonds are only IR inactive in identical chemical environments, since an electronegative neighbour can make them slightly IR active. Higher ppm comes from less shielding, which means more field felt and more energy needed. In the n+1 rule, $n$ counts every non-equivalent proton within 3 bonds across all groups, and it gives lines inside one signal. DoU = 0 means no rings or π bonds. The molecular dipole is the vector sum of the bond dipoles. The column row tactic says different rows means reduced mass dominates, while the same row means just compare electronegativity.
