## Summary

Quiz review notes pulling together the IR, VSEPR and electronegativity material. On IR, the wavenumber axis is governed by reduced mass and the intensity axis by polarity. On geometry, counting electron domains gives the shape — 4 domains is tetrahedral, and 4 domains with 1 lone pair and 3 bonds is trigonal pyramidal — and whether the bond dipoles cancel depends on whether the surrounding atoms are identical, since lone pairs or mixed atoms break the cancellation. On electronegativity, delta electronegativity is useful for direction and comparison but isn't equivalent or proportional to partial charge, and the scale itself is arbitrary with F pinned at 4.00 as the benchmark. The general problem-solving recipe is: total valence vs required valence to get bonding and lone electrons, then per-atom bond and lone pair counts to drive formal charge toward 0, then VSEPR for the shape. The last note — that more partial charge means more ionic character and a higher BDE — is the one to double-check, since the H-F/H-Cl example is written the wrong way round.
