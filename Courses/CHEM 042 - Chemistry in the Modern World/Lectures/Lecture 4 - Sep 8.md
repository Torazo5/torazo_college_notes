## Summary

This lecture swapped mass spec for absorption spectroscopy as the way to find composition: sweep the frequency of the radiation source (so photon energy changes, since $E = hf$), then look at which frequencies go missing at the detector — those are the ones that got absorbed into a quantum energy level. Quantitatively that's read off $\frac{I}{I_{0}}$, where $I_{0}$ is the incident intensity, so a lower ratio means something absorbed. The reason absorption happens at particular frequencies comes from the bond potential energy curve — equilibrium sits at the local minimum, and stretching or compressing raises the potential energy — and the energy needed to excite that vibration depends on two things: the force constant (bond strength) and the reduced mass (mass of the atoms). The practice problems used that to compare a double vs. triple bond, where the triple bond is the stronger one.
