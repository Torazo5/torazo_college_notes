## Summary

Chromatography is about separating the components of a mixture, which mass spec on its own can't do cleanly. You inject a mobile phase (inert gas or liquid solvent) that the sample mixes into, and the components interact with a stationary phase, usually silica. Each component has a different affinity for the mobile vs. stationary phase, so they migrate at different rates and come apart, and then you detect them. The one that barely moves (green in the image) has the highest affinity for the stationary phase. For this to work, the mixture has to exist in the same phase as the mobile phase and its components need different strengths of attraction to the stationary phase. That attraction is probably just the intermolecular forces from the reading, like hydrogen bonding.
