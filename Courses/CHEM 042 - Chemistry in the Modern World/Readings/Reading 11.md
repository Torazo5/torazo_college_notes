## Practice

**Q1.** Given the overall reaction and rate law, what is true of the proposed mechanism?

Overall: $2\,\text{NO}(g) + 2\,\text{H}_2(g) \to \text{N}_2(g) + 2\,\text{H}_2\text{O}(g)$
Rate law: $\text{Rate} = k_{obs}[\text{NO}]^2[\text{H}_2]$

- Step 1: $2\,\text{NO}(g) + \text{H}_2(g) \to \text{N}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g)$
- Step 2: $\text{N}_2\text{O}(g) + \text{H}_2(g) \to \text{N}_2(g) + \text{H}_2\text{O}(g)$

A. Step 1 is the rate-determining step and has the lowest activation energy
B. Step 1 is the rate-determining step and has the highest activation energy
C. Step 2 is the rate-determining step and has the lowest activation energy
D. Step 2 is the rate-determining step and has the highest activation energy

> [!success]- Answer: B
> - step 1 rate law (elementary → read off coefficients): $k_1[\text{NO}]^2[\text{H}_2]$ → matches experimental, so step 1 is rate-determining
> - step 2 would give $k_2[\text{N}_2\text{O}][\text{H}_2]$ → has an intermediate, doesn't match
> - rate-determining = slowest → smallest $k$ → **highest** activation energy (A contradicts itself: lowest barrier = fastest step, can't be the bottleneck)
> - side note: step 1 is termolecular (3 molecules colliding at once), part of why it's slow

**Q2.** Determine the rate law for the overall reaction based on this mechanism:

Overall: $2A + 2B \to D + F$

- Step 1: $2A + B \rightleftharpoons C$ (fast and reversible)
- Step 2: $B + C \to D + E$ (slow)
- Step 3: $E \to F$ (fast)

A. $\text{Rate} = k_2[B][C]$
B. $\text{Rate} = k_1[A]^2[B]$
C. $\text{Rate} = \dfrac{k_2}{k_1}[A]^2[B]^2$
D. $\text{Rate} = \dfrac{k_2k_1}{k_{-1}}[A]^2[B]^2$

> [!success]- Answer: D
> 1. start from the slow step: $\text{Rate} = k_2[B][C]$ → but $C$ is an intermediate, so A can't be the final answer
> 2. step 1 is fast and reversible → it reaches equilibrium (forward rate = reverse rate):
> $$k_1[A]^2[B] = k_{-1}[C] \quad\Rightarrow\quad [C] = \frac{k_1}{k_{-1}}[A]^2[B]$$
> 3. substitute into the slow step:
> $$\text{Rate} = k_2[B]\cdot\frac{k_1}{k_{-1}}[A]^2[B] = \frac{k_2k_1}{k_{-1}}[A]^2[B]^2$$
> - why "fast and reversible" matters: step 1 goes back and forth way faster than step 2 uses up $C$, so it has time to sit at equilibrium. that's what lets us write $[C]$ in terms of $[A]$ and $[B]$
> - B uses the wrong step; C has $k_1$ flipped and is missing $k_{-1}$
