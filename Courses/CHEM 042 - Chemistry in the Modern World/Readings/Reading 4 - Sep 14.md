## Summary

This reading defines a saturated compound as one with no rings and only single bonds, which for a hydrocarbon means it follows $C_{n}H_{2n+2}$. From there, the degree of unsaturation is how far short of saturation a molecule actually is: work out how many hydrogens the saturated version would have, take the difference from what you've got, and divide by 2. Each unit of DoU accounts for one extra bond or one extra ring — so DoU 1 means either a double bond or a ring, and DoU 2 means a triple bond, two double bonds, a double bond plus a ring, or two rings. For non-hydrocarbons you first normalize the formula before applying the same logic: drop the oxygens, convert any halogen to a hydrogen, and remove nitrogen along with a hydrogen.
