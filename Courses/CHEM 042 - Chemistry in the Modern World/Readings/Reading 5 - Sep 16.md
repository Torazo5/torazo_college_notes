## Summary

This reading is about induction — the long-range effect of a bond dipole created by electronegative atoms, and explicitly not the induction from physics. A strongly electronegative atom like fluorine pulls electron density off the carbon it's bonded to, that carbon then pulls from the next one, and the effect propagates down the chain as an overall dipole, which makes F an inductively withdrawing group. A molecule without that group has much less polarized bonds and generally higher electron density everywhere, since nothing is pulling it away. The key practical point is how fast the effect dies: it weakens significantly with separation, and the thing to count is the number of bonds to get there rather than the distance on the diagram — so even three fluorines a few bonds away lose most of their influence on a given proton. Bond dipole arrows point toward the more electronegative atom.
