## Summary

Electronegativity is just the tendency of atoms attracting electrons, and it's what sorts bonds into categories: covalent (shared electrons — nonpolar if even, polar if uneven) versus ionic (complete transfer to the more electronegative atom). Pauling's empirical scale exists because bonds like H-F are stronger than you'd expect from the pure covalent picture, and the explanation is partial charge separation — F pulls the electron toward itself so F goes partially negative and H partially positive, and that attraction adds strength. That extra strength gets quantified as the additional bond energy, $BDE_{H-F} - \sqrt{ BDE_{H-H} * BDE_{F-F}}$, which converts into an electronegativity difference via $0.102\sqrt{ \Delta }$ (1.78 for HF). Where that difference lands then tells you the bond type: 0–0.4 non-polar covalent, 0.4–1.9 polar covalent, 1.9–3.3 ionic. Trend-wise, more valence electrons generally means more electronegativity (Hydrogen being the anomaly), increasing by period and by column.
