$$
D(f \circ x)=Df(x(a))Dx(a)

$$
m(x,y)=(x,y,z(x,y))
$$
This makes sense because realistically the only two input variables are x and y as z is just a certain function on them. And also the outer function needs to get 3 variables and output one(0), so this makes logical sense.
Here, I call it $m(x,y)$ as calling it $x(a)$ like in the formula may cause confusion in the algebra.
thus now we can proceed with the formula:
$$
Dm(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}
1&0 \\
0&1 \\
z_{x}&z_{y}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
$$
DF(m(x,y))=(F_{x}(x,y,z),F_{y}(x,y,z),F_{z}(x,y,z))
$$
And as mentioned above as well as in the question, we know that $D(f \circ x)=0$ for the output is always 0 and a constant would mean no change. 
Thus we know this is true now:
$$
0=(F_{x}(x,y,z),F_{y}(x,y,z),F_{z}(x,y,z))\begin{pmatrix}
1&0 \\
0&1 \\
z_{x}&z_{y}
\end{pmatrix}
$$
Doing matrix multiplication we know this is true:
$$
F_{x}(x,y,z)+F_{z}(x,y,z)\cdot z_{x}=0
$$
Which can be re-arranged to show:
$$
\boxed{z_{x}=-\frac{F_{x}(x,y,z)}{F_{z}(x,y,z)} = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}}

this is with the assumption that $F_{z} \neq{0}$
Another equation from the matrix multiplication shows:
$$
F_{y}(x,y,z)+F_{z}(x,y,z)\cdot z_{y}=0

re-arranging:
