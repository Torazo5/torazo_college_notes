# Dot product

> [!note] Partly written by Claudian
> Your notes only had the formula, not what the dot product is, so the definition above is filled in. Your original is kept below. Check the completion against the Sep 2 lecture.

a way to multiply two vectors and get a single number (a scalar): multiply matching components and add, $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{b} = a_1b_1 + a_2b_2 + a_3b_3$. Geometrically it equals $\|\mathbf{a}\|\,\|\mathbf{b}\|\cos\theta$, so it measures how much two vectors point the same way. It's $0$ exactly when they're perpendicular, and $\mathbf{a}\cdot\mathbf{a} = \|\mathbf{a}\|^2$.
